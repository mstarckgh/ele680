# 24-hour-ahead electricity load forecasting: recurrent vs. convolutional networks and the role of a seasonal baseline

## 1 Introduction

Electricity load has strong daily and weekly cycles, so a one-day-ahead forecast benefits from information up to a week or more back. Bai et al. [1] argue that generic convolutional networks (TCN) match or beat recurrent networks on sequence tasks, and Lai et al. [2] discuss why recurrent models struggle with long periodic patterns on this same dataset. We revisit that comparison on the UCI ElectricityLoadDiagrams20112014 data with a one-shot 24 h forecast, using LSTM, GRU and TCN models of matched size.

Our first contribution is a negative result that shaped the rest of the work: with the standard setup, **all three networks lose to the trivial forecast "repeat last week"**. Our second contribution is a residual formulation in which the network predicts only a correction to that seasonal-naive forecast; this closes the gap and, for the TCN, beats the baseline. ## 2 Methods and materials

**Data.** We use 10 of the 370 clients of the UCI dataset (MT_156, 159, 169, 232, 242, 248, 266, 281, 302, 307), drawn once with a fixed seed among clients with no zero-load hours since 2012 (`configs/clients.json`). The 15-minute readings are averaged to hourly values. The series from 2012-01-01 is split chronologically 70/10/20 (train/validation/test); z-scoring uses training statistics only, and windows never cross split boundaries. Validation and test windows start at midnight so every forecast covers one calendar day.

**Task.** Input: the last L hours of all 10 clients (shape L x 10). Output: the next 24 hours of all clients in one shot (24 x 10), via a linear head on the last time step. Metrics (in kW, after undoing normalisation): MAE (primary), RMSE, RSE, CORR, and WAPE per client. We also report MAE with the two days affected by the October 2014 clock change excluded ("noDST"), because the doubled 01:00 hour disturbs the one-week-lag baseline.

**Models** (all about 68k parameters so that capacity is comparable):
- LSTM: 2 layers, 64 hidden units (68,336 parameters).
- GRU: 2 layers, 72 hidden units (67,200).
- TCN: 7 residual blocks with two weight-normalised causal dilated convolutions each, kernel 3, dilations 1, 2, ..., 64, 38 channels (68,298). Receptive field 1 + 4(2^n - 1) = 509 h for n = 7. The TCN is implemented from scratch following [1].

**Training.** Adam (lr 1e-3, halved on validation plateau), batch size 64, MSE on normalised data, gradient clipping at 1.0, dropout 0.1, at most 40 epochs with early stopping (patience 6) on validation MSE. Every configuration is trained with 5 seeds and we report the mean and standard deviation of the test metrics. Experiment configurations are generated by `scripts/make_configs.py`.

**Baselines.** naive-24h (repeat yesterday) and naive-168h (repeat the same day of last week).

**Residual variant.** The network output is added to the load of the same 24 hours one week earlier, taken from the input window (needs L >= 168). The net therefore only has to learn deviations from the weekly pattern.

**Ablations.** (i) L1 instead of MSE loss; (ii) residual; (iii) residual + L1; (iv) input length L; (v) TCN receptive field (depth).

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Plain models lose to the seasonal baseline

Test MAE in kW, L = 168, mean +- std over 5 seeds:

| model | MAE | RMSE |
|---|---|---|
| naive-24h | 14.46 | 30.7 |
| naive-168h | 14.43 | 28.3 |
| LSTM | 17.87 +- 0.45 | 31.4 |
| GRU | 16.49 +- 0.25 | 29.7 |
| TCN | 16.58 +- 0.34 | 30.1 |

LSTM is clearly worst; GRU and TCN are indistinguishable within seed noise. All are worse than naive-168h. The gap is concentrated in client MT_159 (mean load 44 kW, WAPE 45-55% for the plain nets against 30% for naive-168h); on most other clients the plain nets are within 1-2 WAPE points of the baseline.

### 3.2 Input length (plain models)

Figure `figures/input_length.pdf`. Longer inputs do not help systematically: GRU is best at L = 24 (16.50) and worse at L = 336 (17.25); TCN improves up to L = 336 (16.72) and degrades again at 672 (17.14); LSTM is worst everywhere (17.3-18.3). This is consistent with the networks not exploiting the weekly cycle, even when it is visible in the window.

### 3.3 Loss and residual formulation (L = 168)

| variant | GRU | TCN |
|---|---|---|
| plain (MSE) | 16.49 | 16.58 |
| L1 loss | 16.12 +- 0.29 | 16.42 +- 0.24 |
| residual | 14.49 +- 0.13 | 13.87 +- 0.13 |
| residual + L1 | 14.50 +- 0.20 | 13.79 +- 0.15 |
| naive-168h | 14.43 | 14.43 |

L1 loss helps the plain models slightly. The residual formulation helps far more: the GRU reaches the level of the baseline and the TCN improves on it by about 0.55 kW (4%), roughly four seed standard deviations. Adding L1 on top of the residual changes nothing. The advantage of the TCN over the GRU appears only in the residual setting. Training is also much faster with the residual (GRU: 9 epochs against 19).

## 4 Discussion

**Why do plain networks lose?** Seasonal-naive-168h is a strong baseline for this data: load is dominated by weekly repetition. A plain network must reproduce the previous week's values from normalised inputs through a bottleneck (the last hidden state or the last TCN time step, 38-72 units) before the head, which costs accuracy on the part of the signal that is easiest to predict. Giving the weekly lag directly as a skip removes that burden. TODO: confirm with per-horizon / per-weekday error breakdown (weekend vs weekday errors are available in `results/analysis_*.txt`).

**Recurrent vs. convolutional.** Without the residual we do not reproduce a clear advantage of the TCN over the GRU as claimed in [1]; we do see LSTM < GRU. With the residual the TCN is better than the GRU. A possible reading is that, once the periodic part is given, the TCN's direct access to many past time steps (fixed receptive field, no gating) is better suited to learn the remaining corrections. This is a hypothesis; the L = 672 and receptive-field experiments are meant to test it. TODO.

**Limitations.** 10 clients only; one chronological split; a single hyperparameter setting per model, with capacity matched by parameter count instead of tuned; the TCN gain over the baseline is small in absolute terms; client MT_159 dominates the error of the plain models. The DST days are excluded in a secondary metric only.
